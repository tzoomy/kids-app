## Summary

The last level of the Workshop. The whole town still sleeps. The child sets up **four placements** — the ramp, two dominoes and the scissors — then presses Play. Play does one thing: it releases the ball (the level's one start object). Everything after is **one causal chain**: ball → ramp ↘ → cup → domino row → Coco's small bell (the friends stir and fall back asleep: cosmetic, **not** a goal) → the last domino lands on the **lever** → the **cord** closes the scissors on the string she chose → the sandbag drops on the seesaw → the hammer hits the **big morning bell**. That bell is the only goal. Ringing it wakes the town: six friends wake in their windows and wave, the town crossfades to morning, and the **egg piece** (the final shell piece that completes the egg) flies to the nest.

The scissors **never snip at Play** (decision 5, closed): they close only when the lever trips and the cord pulls them. With no scissors the cord pulls an empty hook; on the wrong string (B) the lantern drops instead of the sandbag.

The level is reachable by the preview route `?level=20` (or `#/workshop/20`), independent of progress; without it the app boots exactly as before.

## Learning goals

- **Age 5:** planning one long cause-and-effect chain (ramp → dominoes → lever → scissors → bell), spatial reasoning (where a domino row has no big hole, which string holds the sack), persistence after a failed run, French listening (« Bonjour, Ville des Surprises ! »). The Hebrew robot hints explain the goal and name the problem, never the solution until the 3rd failed run (a glowing socket).
- **Age 2:** none (the Workshop is the age-5 world).

## World and why

The Machine Workshop, level 20 of 20. Until the other 19 levels exist the level is entered only by the preview route; where it sits on the map afterwards is an open decision (`docs/PENDING-DECISIONS.md`).

## Mechanic

**Board** (world px, 1280×800, y down; `src/games/mysteryEgg/workshop/l20Level.ts` is the single source, the physics and the scene both read it; every number is the v5.1 sim's `X.L20`): ceiling y 40–60, walls x 20–40 / 1250–1270, floor top y 700. The tray is a UI strip below the floor (y 700–800).

- **Low machine** (L5/L6 geometry): chute x 200 (walls ±42–48, y 40–150); ramp socket **R1** (200,440); the sunk cup; the domino table x **390–1150** (extended in v5.1), top 560; fixed dominoes 400, 540, **710**, 780, 860, 940 (710 not 700: the fallen 580 domino's tip reaches x 690, the left face of a domino at 700); **strip slots 460 / 500 / 580 / 620** for the child's two dominoes (540 is pre-placed); Coco's small bell (1050,470), a sensor circle r 44 (cosmetic mid-chain bell).
- **Lever (the shared v5.1 lever, `workshop/lever.ts`, one spec for L13/L14/L15/L20):** a dynamic plank 160 × 10, density 0.001, pinned by a length-0 constraint on a static post at **pivot (1125,548)** (moved from the approved (1140,545), where domino 940 tripped it in only 4–6 of 10–20 runs: decision 19, accepted by Maor 2026-11-28), post box x 1119–1131, y 558–560. It rests **11° left-up** (right end down): left tip ≈ (1046.5, 532.7) — still below Coco's sensor (bottom y 514), so the lever never rings it — right tip ≈ (1203.5, 563.3). A **counterweight** circle r 6, mass 1.2, sits at the right tip and rests on a static **stop** (box x 1192.5–1214.5, y 568.3–578.3 under the tip); a second static **down-stop** under the left arm (box 16 × 10 at the left tip's position after 30° of turn) limits the turn to 30°. It stays within 1° of its rest angle during the 60 settle steps in every run and **trips at ≥ 10°** from its angle at the first run step. It is hit from above by domino 940.
- **Cord link (scripted, no Matter body):** a drawn polyline lever right tip → P0 (1238,688) → P1 (1244,72) → P2 (896,72) → the scissors handle H (880,98). On the first step the lever is ≥ 10° from its start it fires once: `lever:tripped`, then, if the scissors sit on string A or B, that string's constraint is removed and `snip:A|B` follows; with an empty socket only `lever:tripped` fires ("the cord pulled an empty hook"). The cord goes taut/animates on the trip (a bright pulse along the polyline, `sfx-ws-rope-swish`) and the scissors close (`ws-scissors-snip`, `sfx-ws-snip`).
- **High machine:** a seesaw (compound body, pivot (1000,340), arms 160/160, thickness 16, hanging from a ceiling bracket x 996–1004, **starting right-end-down on a ledge x 1150–1228, top 408, 20 px thick** at asin(68/160) ≈ 25°); the **sandbag** (mass 24, r 36, at (860,200)) on **string A** from the ceiling (60→200, length 140) above the left end, with a sand tray (x 720–900, y 420, lip x 720–732) under it; a **lantern** (mass 3, r 24, at (640,220)) on **string B** (length 160), the decoy; the **morning bell** (1150,230), sensor r 44. The stop shelf x 1182–1250 and the bracket down to y 338 in the fixtures art are decor only, not physics.
- **Parts (tray):** the ramp (arrives down-right ↘; a tap on the placed ramp flips ↘/↙), two dominoes, the scissors.
- **Typed sockets (v4 §1.4):** R1 accepts the ramp only; the four strip slots accept dominoes only; **string A (860,110)** and **string B (640,110)** accept the scissors only. A part dropped on a socket that does not accept it springs back to the tray with `{accepted:false, reason}` (`wrong-part`, `occupied`, `too-far`); a placed part can be dragged off. The scissors sit at the **top** of the string (y 110, under the ceiling), never over the sack (this fixes the mockup mismatch).
- **Play (a fresh engine every time, v4 §1.1):** the world is rebuilt from level data at **`engine.timing.timeScale = 1.25`** with the **v5.1 resolver pin** (`_restingThresh 4`, `_restingThreshTangent 6`, `_positionDampen 0.9`, `_positionWarming 0.8`, `_frictionNormalMultiplier 5` — the Phaser plugin's values, set around every step and restored; `physicsConfig.ts`), 60 settle steps run, then the ball is released from the chute at (200,95) with zero speed. **Nothing is cut at Play.** Fixed step 1/60 s. A run ends when every body has rested 1 s, or at the timeout (16 s).
- **Bells:** a bell rings once when a moving body (speed ≥ 0.2 or angular speed ≥ 0.01) touches its sensor, and records which body did. Coco's small bell is cosmetic (the friends stir: the existing twitch; they do not wake). The morning bell is the goal.
- **Win (one goal, `goal:morning`):** the morning bell is rung **by the seesaw**, Coco's bell **by a domino**, and the order holds: `bell:coco` ≤ `snip` < `goal:morning` (steps). Events in order: `bell:coco:dom<x>` → `lever:tripped` → `snip:A` → `goal:morning` (names not yet approved: decision 9).
- **Solution:** ramp ↘ in R1, dominoes in {460, 620} or {500, 620}, scissors on A. At timeScale 1.25 the morning bell rings at a median **6.17 s** ({460,620}) / **6.25 s** ({500,620}), p95 6.33 / 6.55 s (v4 at 1.0×: 8.48 s). **Wrong:** a wrong domino pair ({460,500}, {580,620}, {500,580}, {460,580}) → the row stops, no small bell, no lever; ramp ↙ or none → the ball misses the cup; scissors on B → the lantern drops (it lands on the table, y ≈ 536), no big bell; no scissors → the cord yanks an empty hook; an empty board.
- **Town:** six windows (110×110) with their friends (busts 92×92) behind the machines: moustache (300,195), bruno (460,195), pip (760,195), grandpere (300,325), cocopom (460,325), plume (620,325). They breathe while asleep and twitch (cosmetic) when Coco's small bell rings during the run (they stay asleep). The egg nest (centre (380,98), scale 0.35) and the capybara (feet on the floor near (120,616)) are on the board.
- **Win finale (after the run ends):** (the morning bell rang by the seesaw) staggered wake (150 ms per window: squash-stretch pop, asleep → awake, wave ±8°), `vo-fr-ws-l20-bonjour`, crossfade dawn → morning 1.2 s with `sfx-ws-town-wake`, confetti, the capybara cheers, the egg glows and wiggles, `ws-egg-piece` flies from the morning bell to the nest, `vo-fr-ws-l20-success`, optional `vo-he-ws-egg-piece`, `sfx-success`; the profile's egg piece is saved **once** (see Persistence); then a Continue button returns to the map.
- **Failure (no buzzer, ever):** Coco's small bell had stirred the friends but the chain failed → they fall asleep again (droop tween, swap back), the capybara "rings an imaginary bell", `sfx-ws-yawn`, and `vo-fr-ws-l20-one-bell` (Moustache) or `vo-fr-presque`. The small bell never rang → the capybara mime (facepalm) and a hint. Run reasons: `lever-not-hit`, `empty-hook`, `wrong-string`, `no-goal` (`goal` = the win). The board then resets for another try (parts stay placed).
- **Hint ladder (v4 §1.8, reordered by the team lead):** the robot ear button, and the 2nd failed run → the goal hint `vo-he-ws-l20-goal` (never the solution); a failure where the domino row stopped → `vo-he-ws-dominoes-gap`; where the lever tripped but there was no goal (empty hook, wrong string) → `vo-he-ws-scissors-which`; nothing reached the lever → no targeted hint. Then the ladder steps (`hintRung` = the **ladder step**, i.e. the failed-run count up to 6; v4's own rung numbers are not used): **3rd failure → a glowing socket** (the ramp first, with `vo-he-robot-help-2`, then the scissors on A, then a domino slot) and `vo-he-robot-breathe` once; **4th failure → the trajectory** (v4 "rung 2": a dotted path of where the ball would go with the CURRENT setup; see below), announced by `sfx-robot-beep-1` only, no voice line (`vo-fr-robot-regarde` is kept for the ghost so it is never spoken twice in a row); **5th failure → the ghost**: a translucent ghost of the next needed part, in the right orientation, pulsing in its socket until that part is placed correctly, with `vo-fr-robot-regarde` (« Regarde ici ! »); **6th and later failures → Bip places ONE correct part** (the same "next needed" part) with a visible tween from the tray (or from a wrong socket, which it frees first), `sfx-robot-beep-1` and `vo-fr-robot-aide` (« Je t'aide un peu ! »); one part per step-6 failure, never Play, the rest stays hers. Later steps keep the earlier visual aids: the glowing socket stays until its part is placed, and the trajectory stays on through the ghost and Bip's steps. **Next needed part** (shared by steps 3, 5 and 6, `nextNeeded` in `l20Hints.ts`): the ramp down-right in R1; then the scissors on string A; then the domino slot that completes {460,620} or {500,620} given what is placed (a domino from the tray is used before one is lifted from a wrong slot). With nothing left to fix, the ghost and Bip do nothing.
- **Trajectory preview (ladder step 4 and later, cosmetic):** `trajectoryFor` (`l20Trajectory.ts`, pure, Phaser-free) builds a fresh headless `L20World` from the CURRENT placements, with the same config and 60 settle steps as Play (the lever's cord does the snip), steps it at the fixed 1/60 s step (timeScale 1.25, resolver pin) until the run ends (rest 1 s or the 16 s timeout) and samples the BALL every 5 steps (a sample is kept only if the ball moved 3 px, so a resting ball adds none; the final resting position is always the last point). Deterministic (no jitter) and cached by config. The scene draws evenly spaced soft dots (30 px apart, palette indigo with a white halo, fading from 0.95 to 0.45 alpha along the path) behind the placed parts (depth 12.5); when she places, moves or flips a part during the build phase the path is recomputed and redrawn after 200 ms of calm (never every drag frame, and not while a part is held). The dots hide when Play is tapped and while the run, the feedback and the win play; they come back when the board resets after a failure. It never touches the live physics.
- **Break-point marker (cosmetic):** after every failed run a pulsing circle (code-drawn) shows where the chain first stopped (`chooseBreakPoint` in `l20BreakPoint.ts`, pure): the R1 socket when the ball missed the ramp, rolled the wrong way or no domino tipped; the centre of the strip gap between the last fallen and the first standing domino when the row stopped; the lever's left arm when the whole row fell but the lever did not trip ("lever not hit"); the string-A hook when the lever tripped with no scissors ("the cord pulled an empty hook"); the string-B socket when the scissors were on B ("wrong string", the lantern dropped); the morning bell when the right string was cut and the bell still stayed silent. It hides when the board is touched (a part is pressed, Bip places a part) or Play is tapped.
- **Intro:** `vo-fr-ws-l20-story`, then the goal hint (a Play cuts it).

**Media:** Gemini's v5.1 files are on the branch (`public/art/mystery-egg`, `public/audio/mystery-egg`: 50 keys incl. `ws-l20-cord`, `ws-lever`, `ws-lever-base`) and the scene uses them. Z-order: background < fixtures < cord < props. `ws-lever` (200×32): image centre = the pin (1125,548), rotated with the lever body. `ws-lever-base` (120×40): its pin at display (20,6) sits on (1125,548); the right stop is baked for a 160 px plank at 11° (stop centre = pin + (78.5, 25.3)). `ws-l20-cord` is the static part P0 → P1 → P2 → H; the lever-tip → P0 leg is drawn live every frame with the `ws-rope` tile. The scissors face left on the board (`flipX`), so the handle anchors the cord at H. Placeholders remain only where there is no file: the code-drawn cord pulse, the socket markers, the break-point circle. Every key still has a code-drawn / synth fallback; a missing file never breaks the level.

## Persistence

The egg piece is the **final shell piece that completes the egg** (team-lead decision, 2026-11-27). It is an additive field `eggPiece: true` of the v2 Mystery Egg save (present only once earned, so older saves and builds are untouched; the save version is not bumped), set once on the first win; it gives the egg its full glow level (`eggLevel` = 5). The feather and the world pieces of older saves are untouched.
